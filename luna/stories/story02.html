LUNA'S MAGICAL WORLD

Collection 1 — Luna and the Amazing Farm

Story 02 — A Sound No One Heard

The little light was gone.

Luna remained beside the bedroom window long after it disappeared. Moonlight rested on the ancient tree beside the river, turning its branches silver. The farm looked peaceful, almost ordinary.

But Luna knew better.

She had seen the light.

And she had heard the knock.

Garo lifted his head from his blanket.

Luna whispered, “Did you hear it too?”

The dog stood.

She slipped on her sweater. “Quietly.”

They went downstairs and crossed the sleeping yard. The pond reflected the moon. The barn was dark. At the river, Luna stopped.

Knock.

The sound came from the ancient tree.

Garo stared at the trunk.

Luna approached. The bark was deeply cracked, and its roots rose from the earth like enormous fingers. She placed her palm against it.

Warm.

She pulled her hand away.

“Trees aren't supposed to be warm.”

Garo began circling the trunk. Behind it, a small patch of earth looked disturbed.

Luna knelt. The soil was warm too.

Then the branches moved.

There was no wind.

Knock.

This time the sound seemed to come from inside the trunk.

Luna pressed her ear against the bark.

For a few seconds she heard only the river.

Then something faint vibrated beneath the wood.

Knock.

She stepped away.

Garo suddenly ran toward the river. Luna followed and found a pale piece of wood between two stones. It was smooth, almost polished, with a dark symbol burned into one side.

Before she could examine it, movement appeared in the reeds across the water.

Someone was there.

“Hello?”

Nothing answered.

Garo growled.

Luna caught his collar. “We're going home.”

At the kitchen door, Grandmother was waiting.

Her eyes went directly to Luna's closed hand.

“What did you find?”

Luna opened it.

Grandmother did not touch the wood.

“Put it away.”

“How did you know it was there?”

“I know that look.”

“Grandmother, did you hear the tree?”

A long silence followed.

“What did you hear?”

“Knocking.”

Grandmother looked toward the river.

Then she said, “Go to bed.”

“That isn't an answer.”

“No,” Grandmother replied quietly. “It isn't.”

Luna went upstairs. She placed the pale wood in her drawer.

Garo lay outside her door.

Just before sleep came, Luna heard a final sound.

Knock.

This time it did not come from the tree.

It came from inside the wall beside her bed.

Garo was already standing.

Luna stared at the wall.

Neither of them moved.

Outside, the ancient tree stood perfectly still.
