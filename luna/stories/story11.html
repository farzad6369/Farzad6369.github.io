Story 11

What Grandmother Knew

The morning after the strange light appeared near the old tree, Luna woke before anyone else in the house.

For a few moments she stayed still beneath her blanket, listening.

The farm was quiet, but it did not feel ordinary anymore.

Somewhere outside, a bird called twice. From the kitchen came the soft sound of dishes. Beyond the window, the wind moved through the grass beside the pond.

Luna remembered the mark she had seen the day before.

She also remembered the strange feeling that had followed it, as if the farm itself had been trying to tell her something.

When Luna went outside, she found Grandmother beneath the old apple tree.

“You are early,” Grandmother said.

“I could say the same about you.”

Grandmother smiled. “Some things are easier to notice before the day becomes noisy.”

Together they began collecting apples that had fallen during the night.

For several minutes neither of them spoke.

Then Luna finally asked, “Grandmother, how long have you known that something is different about this farm?”

Grandmother stopped.

An apple rested in her hand.

“Longer than you have been alive,” she answered.

Luna looked at her carefully.

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because knowing a secret before you are ready can be more dangerous than not knowing it at all.”

Before Luna could answer, Garo came running across the grass.

The dog stopped beside them with a small rabbit hiding behind his legs.

Luna looked at the rabbit.

Then she heard a tiny voice.

“The ground smells different today.”

Luna froze.

Grandmother did not look surprised.

“You heard him, didn't you?” she asked quietly.

Luna nodded.

“You knew?”

“I suspected.”

The rabbit suddenly ran toward the barn.

Garo followed.

“Wait!” Luna called.

Grandmother gently touched Luna's arm.

“Let him go. He may be showing you something.”

Inside the old barn, Garo began scratching beneath a loose wooden board.

Luna moved the board aside.

Underneath it was a narrow space filled with dust, dry leaves and a folded piece of yellow paper.

She picked it up.

There was a symbol drawn on the paper.

The same symbol she had seen near the old tree.

Grandmother's expression changed.

“I hoped you would never find that,” she whispered.

“What is it?” Luna asked.

Grandmother did not answer immediately.

Instead, she walked to an old wooden chest in the corner of the barn.

She opened it and removed a faded photograph.

In the picture stood Grandmother when she was young, Grandfather beside her, and another person standing slightly behind them.

But that person's face had been scratched away.

Luna stared at the photograph.

“Who is that?”

“Someone who believed the farm belonged to him.”

“Did it?”

Grandmother shook her head.

“No. But he believed there was something beneath the farm that could make him powerful.”

Luna looked toward the floor.

“What is beneath it?”

“I don't know everything.”

“But you know something.”

Grandmother sighed.

“Your grandfather spent many years protecting this place. He believed the farm was connected to something much older than our family.”

Suddenly, Garo growled.

Someone was standing outside the barn.

A man in a dark coat stood beside the fence.

Grandmother went completely still.

The stranger looked at her.

“You kept it hidden for a long time,” he said.

Grandmother stepped in front of Luna.

“And you came back.”

The stranger looked past her toward Luna.

“So the girl knows now.”

Luna felt a cold feeling run through her hands.

“Who are you?” she asked.

The man did not answer.

Instead, he looked toward the old tree near the river.

“The signs have started again.”

Grandmother's voice became firm.

“Leave this farm.”

The stranger smiled faintly.

“You know I cannot do that.”

Then he turned and walked away along the road.

Luna watched him disappear beyond the trees.

“Grandmother,” she whispered, “what does he want?”

Grandmother looked at the old paper in Luna's hands.

“He wants what your grandfather spent his life trying to protect.”

“What is it?”

Grandmother took a slow breath.

“I don't know. But your grandfather left one thing behind that may help us understand.”

“What?”

Grandmother pointed toward the folded paper.

“A map.”

That evening, while the family prepared dinner, Luna kept thinking about the stranger.

Garo sat beside her chair.

Outside, the animals were unusually quiet.

Even the wind seemed to be waiting.

Later, Luna returned to the barn.

She looked at the old paper again.

At the bottom was a faded sentence written in Grandfather's handwriting:

“When the signs return, find the old map before someone else does.”

Luna folded the paper carefully.

She knew then that the mystery of the farm had only just begun.

And somewhere inside the old house, hidden among things her family had kept for generations, the old map was waiting.
