Story 12

The Old Map

Luna barely slept that night.

The stranger's words kept returning to her mind.

Find the old map before someone else does.

At breakfast, her father was busy preparing bread for the village. The warm smell of fresh dough filled the kitchen, but Luna hardly noticed it.

Her mother placed a cup beside her.

“You look as though you spent the whole night walking through a forest.”

Luna smiled weakly.

“Something like that.”

Garo was lying beneath the table. He lifted his head when Luna mentioned the map.

Then he quietly left the kitchen.

Luna followed him outside.

“Where are you going?” she asked.

Garo stopped near the old storage room behind the house.

He scratched at the wooden door.

Luna opened it.

Inside were boxes filled with old clothes, broken tools, jars, books and things the family had not touched for years.

Dust floated through the sunlight coming from a small window.

“Are you sure this is what you want me to find?” Luna asked.

Garo wagged his tail.

From a shelf came another voice.

“Not there.”

Luna looked up.

A pigeon was sitting on a beam.

“Not there,” the pigeon repeated. “Behind the blue box.”

Luna moved the box.

Behind it was a narrow wooden cabinet.

She pulled open the cabinet.

Inside was a bundle of old letters tied with a faded green ribbon.

There was no map.

But one of the letters had Grandfather's name written on it.

Luna carried the bundle to Grandmother.

Grandmother looked at the letters for a long time.

“I wondered when these would be found.”

“You knew they were here?” Luna asked.

“I knew they existed. I did not know where your grandfather had hidden them.”

Grandmother opened the oldest letter.

The paper was almost brown with age.

“Your grandfather wrote that the farm had four important places,” she said.

“The old tree, the pond, the barn and the bridge.”

Luna remembered them.

“The four places we already know.”

Grandmother shook her head.

“Not exactly. He wrote that each place was connected to another.”

Luna spread the letters across the table.

One described the river.

Another mentioned the old wooden bridge.

The third spoke about a stone beside the pond.

But the final letter contained something different.

A small drawing.

It showed four circles connected by lines.

In the center was the same mysterious symbol Luna had seen near the old tree.

“This is not a map,” Luna said.

Grandmother smiled.

“No. It is the beginning of one.”

That afternoon Luna took Garo and walked toward the pond.

The sky was clear, but the air felt unusually cool.

Near the water, the duck family was searching through the reeds.

“Have you seen anything strange?” Luna asked.

The mother duck looked at her.

“The water moved during the night.”

“The wind?” Luna asked.

“No wind.”

Luna looked at the pond.

Near the far bank, something shiny was caught between two stones.

She climbed into the small boat and pushed it across the water.

Garo remained on the shore, barking nervously.

When Luna reached the stones, she found a small metal disk covered in mud.

On one side was the mysterious symbol.

On the other was a tiny arrow.

Luna turned the disk in her hand.

The arrow pointed toward the forest.

“So that is where the map continues,” she whispered.

A sudden splash came from behind her.

Luna turned.

The boat had begun drifting away from the stones.

She grabbed the oar and pulled hard.

For a moment, the boat would not move.

Then something beneath the water struck the bottom.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Luna froze.

It happened again.

Three slow knocks.

She looked over the side of the boat.

Deep beneath the dark water, a faint golden light appeared.

It vanished almost immediately.

When Luna returned to shore, Grandmother was waiting.

“You found the marker,” she said.

Luna held up the metal disk.

“What is it?”

“Your grandfather called them waystones.”

“How many are there?”

Grandmother looked toward the forest.

“Four.”

“And this is one of them?”

“Yes.”

Luna suddenly understood.

The old drawing had four circles.

Each circle represented a place.

Each place held one marker.

And together, the four markers would reveal something hidden beneath the farm.

Before Luna could ask another question, Garo began barking toward the road.

A dark figure stood far away between the trees.

It was the stranger.

He did not approach.

He simply watched them.

Then he turned and disappeared into the forest.

Grandmother closed her hand around Luna's.

“Now you understand why the map was hidden.”

Luna looked at the metal disk.

“We have to find the other three.”

Grandmother nodded.

“But remember this, Luna. A map does not only tell you where to go.”

“What else does it do?”

“Sometimes it tells you what you should be afraid of finding.”

That evening, Luna placed the first waystone beside Grandfather's old letters.

Four places.

Four markers.

One hidden secret.

And somewhere beyond the forest, someone else was searching for the same thing.

Luna knew the next journey would not be like the first.

This time, she would not simply be discovering the mystery.

She would be racing against someone who already knew part of the truth.
