Story 13

A Sound Beneath the Ground

The next morning, Luna carried the old metal waystone everywhere she went.

She kept it inside her coat pocket, wrapped in a piece of cloth so nobody would notice it.

She had discovered one marker near the pond, but three others were still hidden somewhere around the farm.

Her father was repairing the fence near the vegetable garden when Luna walked past.

“Are you planning something?” he asked.

“Why do you think that?”

“Because you have the same face your grandfather used to make whenever he had found a mystery.”

Luna smiled.

“Maybe I am looking for something.”

Her father laughed.

“Then take Garo with you. He always finds things nobody else can.”

Garo immediately lifted his head.

He seemed to understand.

Instead of searching the forest, Luna decided to inspect the barn first.

The old building had been standing on the farm for longer than anyone could remember.

Its wooden walls creaked whenever the wind blew, and the floor was covered with marks from years of animals walking across it.

Garo entered first.

Luna followed.

At first, everything looked normal.

Then she heard something.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Luna stopped.

Garo stopped too.

“Did you hear that?” Luna whispered.

Garo looked at the floor.

There was another sound.

Knock.

Knock.

This time it was louder.

Luna knelt and placed her hand against the wooden floor.

A faint vibration passed through her fingers.

Something was moving beneath them.

She remembered the golden light she had seen beneath the pond.

“Grandmother!” Luna called.

Grandmother arrived a few minutes later.

She listened to the sound without saying anything.

“Your grandfather heard this once,” she finally said.

“What did he do?”

“He covered the place and told everyone never to dig here.”

Luna looked at the floor.

“Why?”

Grandmother pointed toward one of the beams.

There was a small symbol carved into the wood.

It was exactly the same symbol as the one on the waystone.

“The markers are not only directions,” Grandmother said.

“Then what are they?”

“Keys.”

Luna took the metal disk from her pocket.

When she placed it near the carved symbol, the knocking stopped.

For several seconds the barn became completely silent.

Then the floor moved.

A thin line appeared between two wooden boards.

Garo stepped backward.

Luna slowly lifted the boards.

Underneath was a narrow stone passage.

Cold air came from the darkness.

Grandmother grabbed Luna's hand.

“Not alone.”

“You are coming with me?” Luna asked.

“Of course.”

They lit an old lantern and climbed down.

The passage was not deep, but it was much older than the barn above it.

Stone walls surrounded them.

Small roots pushed through the ceiling.

Garo walked carefully ahead.

After several steps, Luna heard whispering.

She looked around.

“Did you say something?” she asked Grandmother.

Grandmother shook her head.

The whisper came again.

But this time Luna recognized it.

It was not a human voice.

Somewhere in the darkness, a group of mice were talking.

“The old door is awake.”

“Someone is coming.”

“Someone who should not be here.”

Luna stopped.

“What do you mean?”

One of the mice appeared beside a stone.

“Not you.”

“Then who?”

The mouse pointed toward the tunnel behind them.

A distant sound echoed through the passage.

Footsteps.

Grandmother immediately lowered the lantern.

They stood silently.

The footsteps came closer.

Then stopped.

Luna held her breath.

A shadow passed across the entrance of the tunnel.

It was the stranger.

He had followed them.

Grandmother whispered, “We must move.”

They hurried deeper into the passage.

At the end they found a stone door.

Four small circles were carved into its surface.

One circle was glowing faintly.

Luna took out the waystone.

It fit perfectly into the first circle.

The door gave a deep sound.

Not a knock this time.

A heartbeat.

Thump.

Thump.

Thump.

The entire tunnel trembled.

Grandmother looked frightened for the first time.

“Luna, take the waystone out.”

But before Luna could move, the stranger's voice came from behind them.

“Don't.”

Luna turned.

He stood at the other end of the passage.

“You have no idea what that door protects,” he said.

Luna looked at him.

“Then why are you trying to open it?”

The stranger did not answer.

Instead, he looked at Grandmother.

“You should have told her the truth years ago.”

Grandmother stepped forward.

“And you should have stayed away.”

The stone door suddenly flashed with golden light.

The stranger stepped back.

For a moment, nobody moved.

Then the door opened only a few inches.

Warm air flowed from the darkness beyond it.

And Luna heard something that made her heart race.

Her name.

Someone, somewhere beyond the door, whispered:

“Luna.”

She stared into the darkness.

The door slowly closed again.

The waystone became cold in her hand.

Grandmother looked at her.

“Now you know why your grandfather was afraid.”

Luna looked at the four empty circles.

Only one had been filled.

Three markers were still missing.

Whatever was hidden beneath the farm was waiting for all four.

And now someone else knew exactly where the first one was.
