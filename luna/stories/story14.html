Story 14

A Place That Was Not on the Map

The following afternoon, Luna decided not to enter the underground passage again.

There were too many unanswered questions.

Instead, she took Grandfather's old drawing and walked around the farm with Garo.

The drawing showed the four places connected by thin lines, but one corner was covered by a dark stain.

“There must be something underneath that stain,” Luna said.

Garo sniffed the paper and sneezed.

“I agree,” Luna laughed.

They began at the old wooden bridge.

The river was unusually calm.

Several ducks were resting near the reeds, while a pair of rabbits watched Luna from the grass.

“Have you seen anything strange?” Luna asked.

The rabbit nearest to her twitched its nose.

“A path appeared last night.”

“Where?”

“Beyond the blackberry bushes.”

Luna thanked them and followed the narrow trail.

At first it looked like an ordinary animal path.

Then the trees became thicker.

The sound of the farm disappeared behind her.

Garo stopped.

He refused to go farther.

“What is wrong?” Luna asked.

Garo stared into the forest.

There was no growl.

Only fear in his eyes.

Luna took a few more steps.

Then she saw it.

A stone arch stood between two enormous trees.

There was no road beneath it.

No fence.

No sign.

Just an arch covered with moss.

Luna opened Grandfather's drawing.

The four known places were marked clearly.

But the stone arch was nowhere on the map.

“A place that isn't on the map,” she whispered.

Garo barked once.

Luna touched the stone.

It was warm.

A small golden line appeared across its surface.

It formed the mysterious symbol.

Then another line appeared.

It looked like a road.

Luna followed it with her finger.

The line pointed deeper into the forest.

She suddenly heard a voice.

“You shouldn't be here.”

Luna turned quickly.

A young fox stood behind a tree.

“Why not?” Luna asked.

“Because the forest remembers people who enter.”

“What does that mean?”

The fox looked toward the arch.

“Some paths lead somewhere. Others lead to something.”

Luna did not understand.

But before she could ask again, the fox disappeared.

Garo suddenly pulled at Luna's coat.

He wanted her to leave.

She listened.

They returned to the farm just before sunset.

Grandmother was sitting on the porch.

One look at Luna's face told her that something had happened.

“Where did you go?”

Luna showed her the drawing.

“I found a place that isn't on it.”

Grandmother became silent.

“The stone arch?” she asked.

Luna stared at her.

“You know it?”

Grandmother nodded slowly.

“Your grandfather called it the Forgotten Gate.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because I hoped you would never find it.”

Luna sat beside her.

“What is behind it?”

Grandmother looked toward the forest.

“That is the problem. Your grandfather never found out.”

“But he went there?”

“Once.”

“What happened?”

Grandmother lowered her voice.

“He returned before sunrise. He was carrying a small stone and nothing else.”

“Where is the stone?”

Grandmother stood up.

“Come with me.”

Inside the house, she opened a drawer beneath an old sewing table.

From the back of the drawer she removed a small cloth bag.

Inside was a smooth black stone.

The same symbol was carved into it.

Luna touched the stone.

At once, she heard the sound of running water.

Then she saw something in her mind.

A valley.

A tower.

A field covered with silver flowers.

And four lights standing around a dark circle.

Luna pulled her hand away.

“What did you see?” Grandmother asked.

“A place.”

“Where?”

“I don't know.”

Grandmother closed her eyes.

“Then perhaps the stone showed you what the map could not.”

That night, Luna returned to her room and placed the drawing beside the black stone.

Something was different.

The dark stain on the old map had become lighter.

Underneath it, Luna could now see faint writing.

She leaned closer.

The words were almost impossible to read.

“When the fourth marker is found, the hidden road will appear.”

Luna looked toward the window.

Far away, beyond the fields, a tiny golden light appeared in the forest.

Then another.

And another.

Four lights.

She counted them carefully.

One near the pond.

One near the barn.

One beyond the river.

And one deep inside the forest.

The four waystones were calling to one another.

But Luna had only found one.

Someone else might already be looking for the others.

She closed the curtain.

Tomorrow, she would need to discover where the second marker was hidden.
