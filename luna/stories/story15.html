Story 15 — Grandmother's Key

Rain fell all morning.

Luna stayed inside.

By afternoon, Grandmother called her to the bedroom.

She was holding a small wooden key.

Luna stared.

“What is it?”

“Something your grandfather left for you.”

“Why me?”

“Because he believed you would know when to use it.”

Luna took the key.

It was small and old, with the same symbol carved into its handle.

“Where does it open?”

Grandmother did not answer.

“Is it the tree?”

“No.”

“The house?”

“No.”

“Then what?”

Grandmother sat beside her.

“Your grandfather once told me that a key should not be used simply because you have found a lock.”

“Why?”

“Because some locks protect things that are not ready to be opened.”

Luna closed her hand around the key.

“Am I ready?”

Grandmother smiled.

“That is what you must discover.”

She gave Luna a final warning.

“Do not show it to anyone.”

Luna thought of the stranger.

“I understand.”

That evening, she hid the key inside her notebook.

Garo watched.

Later, a soft scratching came from beneath the bed.

Luna looked down.

Nothing.

She moved the blanket.

A loose floorboard had shifted.

Under it was a tiny wooden box.

No lock.

No symbol.

Only one sentence carved into the lid:

For the one who finds the key.

Luna's heart raced.

She did not open it.

Not yet.

She placed the box beside her and looked at the key.

For the first time, she understood why Grandmother had waited.

The key was not the answer.

It was the beginning of a choice.
