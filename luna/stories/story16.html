Story 16

The Little Chest

The little wooden chest sat on Luna's table all night.

She had tried to open it several times, but the lid refused to move.

Inside, she could feel the strange seed.

It was almost as if the seed was waiting for something.

At sunrise, Luna carried the chest downstairs.

Grandmother was already in the kitchen.

“You didn't sleep,” Grandmother said.

“Neither did the chest.”

Grandmother smiled.

“Then perhaps it is time to learn what your grandfather knew.”

She placed the chest on the table.

“There are three things you must remember,” she said.

“First, never plant the seed without knowing where it came from.”

“Second, never open a hidden door alone.”

“And third, never trust a person simply because they know the history of this farm.”

Luna thought about the stranger.

“You mean him.”

Grandmother nodded.

Before they could continue, Luna heard a loud crash outside.

Garo barked.

They rushed to the yard.

A wooden cart had overturned near the garden.

Luna's father was trying to lift one of its wheels.

“Are you hurt?” Luna asked.

“No. The wheel simply broke.”

Then Luna noticed something strange.

There were footprints beside the cart.

They did not belong to her father.

Someone had been standing there recently.

Garo sniffed the ground.

He followed the footprints toward the vegetable garden.

Luna followed him.

Near the fence, the footprints disappeared.

But the soil had been disturbed.

Someone had been digging.

“They were looking for something,” Luna said.

Garo began scratching at the dirt.

Under a thin layer of soil was a small piece of metal.

Luna pulled it out.

It was another symbol.

Not a waystone.

It was a tiny silver leaf.

When Luna brought it close to the little chest, the chest became warm.

A line appeared across its lid.

The same silver leaf appeared beside the old symbol.

Then the lid opened.

Inside was the seed.

But there was something else.

A tiny folded note.

Luna unfolded it.

The handwriting was Grandfather's.

“If the chest opens, the first guardian has chosen.”

Luna looked at Grandmother.

“Guardian of what?”

Grandmother's face became pale.

“I don't know.”

“But you know what the word means.”

Grandmother looked toward the forest.

“Your grandfather believed every generation had one person who could protect the secret.”

“And you were that person?”

“I was supposed to be.”

Luna stared at her.

“Then who is it now?”

Grandmother did not answer.

From the garden came a strange rustling sound.

The animals gathered near the fence.

The rabbit was there.

The pigeon landed on the roof.

A mouse appeared beneath the wooden steps.

Even the old horse walked closer.

Luna heard their voices one after another.

“The chest is open.”

“The seed is awake.”

“The ground remembers.”

Then the horse spoke.

“Someone is coming.”

Luna looked toward the road.

The stranger was walking toward the farm.

He stopped at the gate.

“I only want to talk,” he said.

Grandmother stepped forward.

“You have talked enough.”

The stranger looked at Luna.

“Your grandfather knew what that seed could become.”

Luna held the chest close.

“Then tell me.”

He hesitated.

“I can't.”

“Why?”

“Because I never saw it grow.”

Grandmother looked surprised.

“You were there?”

The stranger nodded.

“Years ago. Before your grandfather hid everything.”

He looked at the little chest.

“That seed was never meant to be planted in ordinary soil.”

“Where, then?” Luna asked.

The stranger pointed toward the river.

“Where the four paths meet.”

Then he turned away.

“If you plant it anywhere else, you may wake something that should remain asleep.”

He left before Luna could ask another question.

That evening, Luna returned to the attic.

She opened Grandfather's letters again.

One page contained a drawing of the farm.

The four waystones were marked.

At the center was a small circle beside the river.

Underneath were four words:

“The seed knows the way.”

Luna looked at the silver seed inside the little chest.

It had begun to glow.

For the first time, she realized that the seed was not simply waiting to be planted.

It was waiting to lead her somewhere.

Outside, Garo barked toward the river.

Luna closed the chest.

Tomorrow she would follow the seed.
