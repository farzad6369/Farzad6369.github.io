Story 17

Something Beneath the Soil

The morning after the little chest opened, Luna decided not to follow the glowing seed immediately.

She had learned something important from Grandmother. Strange things did not always need to be chased. Sometimes they needed to be watched first.

So instead of taking the seed to the river, Luna placed it safely inside the little wooden chest and went outside to help her mother in the garden.

The farm was busy.

Her father was carrying flour toward the kitchen. Her mother was sorting vegetables. A pair of geese argued loudly beside the pond, while the old horse watched them as if he had heard the same argument a hundred times.

Garo was sitting beside the vegetable garden.

He was unusually quiet.

Luna noticed him staring at one particular patch of soil.

“What is it, Garo?” she asked.

The dog lifted his head.

“Something is wrong there.”

Luna looked at the ground.

At first she saw nothing unusual. The soil looked dark and damp from yesterday's rain.

Then a rabbit appeared between two rows of vegetables.

“Don't dig there,” the rabbit warned.

Luna stopped.

“Why?”

The rabbit twitched its nose.

“Because the ground isn't sleeping anymore.”

Luna knelt beside the patch.

There was a small crack in the soil.

She touched it carefully.

The earth felt warm.

“That's strange,” she whispered.

Garo sniffed the ground and stepped backward.

“It smells like rain,” he said, “but there hasn't been rain there.”

Luna called her mother.

Together they examined the garden. Her mother thought a root might have pushed upward, but when she pressed the soil with a small wooden stick, something hard stopped it.

“There is something underneath,” she said.

Luna remembered the seed in the chest.

She wondered whether the two things were connected.

But before she could say anything, a loud noise came from the chicken yard.

The chickens scattered in every direction.

One of them had found a small metal object near the fence and was trying to peck it.

Luna picked it up.

It was an old silver button.

There was a tiny symbol carved into it.

The same symbol was on the little chest.

Luna's heart beat faster.

She ran to find Grandmother.

Grandmother was sitting beneath the old tree, repairing a basket.

When Luna placed the button in her hand, the old woman became completely still.

“Where did you find this?” she asked.

“Near the chicken yard.”

Grandmother turned the button over several times.

“Your grandfather had one like this.”

“What did it belong to?”

Grandmother smiled sadly.

“A coat.”

“Whose coat?”

“A traveler.”

Luna waited.

Grandmother finally continued.

“Many years ago, before your grandfather became the keeper of this farm, a traveler arrived during a terrible storm. He stayed only one night. In the morning he disappeared.”

“Did he leave the button?”

“No,” Grandmother said. “Your grandfather found it months later, buried in the garden.”

Luna looked toward the vegetable patch.

“So there was something buried there before.”

Grandmother nodded.

“Perhaps.”

They returned to the garden together.

The animals had gathered around the strange patch.

The horse stood near the fence. The cat sat on a wooden box. The rabbit watched from beneath a bush. Even the usually noisy geese were silent.

Grandmother carefully loosened the soil.

After a few minutes, the wooden stick struck something.

Clink.

Luna heard it clearly.

They dug a little deeper.

A flat metal box appeared beneath the soil.

It was not large.

There was no lock.

Only a simple drawing was carved on its lid: a tree with four roots.

Luna looked at Grandmother.

“Should we open it?”

Grandmother did not answer immediately.

Then she said, “Not here.”

They carried the box to the farmhouse.

Inside were three things.

A dried leaf.

A small piece of blue cloth.

And a letter wrapped in oil paper.

The handwriting belonged to Grandfather.

Luna carefully unfolded the letter.

It contained only a few lines.

“If this box is found again, the farm has remembered something that we forgot.”

“Do not search for the buried thing with fear.”

“Search with patience.”

Luna read the words twice.

Then Garo barked.

Everyone turned toward the window.

Outside, the soil in the garden had begun to move.

A tiny green shoot pushed through the earth.

It was not like any plant Luna had ever seen.

Its stem was silver at first, then slowly became green.

Two small leaves unfolded.

As the sunlight touched them, a faint golden line appeared along their edges.

The animals moved closer.

The rabbit whispered, “It knows this place.”

Luna remembered the glowing seed inside the little chest.

She ran upstairs.

When she opened the chest, the seed was no longer glowing.

Instead, it had changed.

A tiny root had appeared beneath it.

Luna stared at it in amazement.

“You were waiting for the soil,” she whispered.

Grandmother stood behind her.

“Perhaps,” she said.

“What happens now?” Luna asked.

Grandmother looked toward the garden.

“Now we wait.”

That evening, Luna sat beside the new plant while the sun disappeared behind the trees.

Garo lay beside her.

The farm was quiet.

For the first time, Luna understood that the mystery was not simply hidden beneath the farm.

It was growing with it.

And somewhere beneath the ordinary soil, something very old had finally begun to wake.

Luna looked at the little green plant.

One of its leaves slowly turned toward the farmhouse.

Not toward the river.

Not toward the forest.

Toward the little chest upstairs.

Luna smiled.

“I think tomorrow is going to be interesting.”
