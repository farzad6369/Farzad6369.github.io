Story 18

Seeds with a Strange Mark

The next morning, Luna woke before the sun had reached the windows.

She went straight to the garden.

The little plant from yesterday had changed again.

It was taller now, with three silver-edged leaves and a thin stem that seemed almost too delicate to hold them.

Garo was already sitting beside it.

“You stayed here all night?” Luna asked.

Garo wagged his tail.

“Someone had to guard it.”

Luna smiled.

She touched one of the leaves.

It was warm.

Then she heard a tiny voice behind her.

“Luna.”

She turned.

The mouse was standing near the garden wall.

“What happened?” Luna asked.

The mouse pointed toward the storage shed.

“There are seeds there that smell like this plant.”

Luna frowned.

“Seeds?”

“Many seeds.”

Luna called Garo and walked toward the shed.

The wooden door was half open.

Inside were baskets of old farm supplies, jars, ropes, tools, and several wooden boxes that had not been touched for years.

Her mother was already there.

She was sorting old gardening tools.

“Good morning,” she said. “I was looking for the small hand shovel.”

Luna told her about the seeds.

Her mother laughed softly.

“There are probably hundreds of seeds here. Your grandfather saved everything.”

They began searching through the boxes.

One box contained beans.

Another contained pumpkin seeds.

A third contained dried corn.

Then Luna noticed a small cloth bag at the bottom of an old basket.

It was tied with blue thread.

The same color as the cloth they had found in the metal box.

Luna carefully opened it.

Inside were twelve seeds.

They were pale brown, but each one had a tiny silver mark.

“I've never seen seeds like these,” Luna said.

Her mother leaned closer.

“Neither have I.”

Garo sniffed the bag.

He sneezed.

“They smell like the garden.”

Luna looked at the seeds.

Each silver mark looked different.

One looked like a leaf.

One looked like a wave.

One looked like a bird.

Another looked like a small star.

And one looked exactly like the symbol carved into the little chest.

Luna's mother noticed it too.

“Perhaps we should show these to Grandmother.”

They carried the bag to the kitchen.

Grandmother was making bread with Luna's father.

When she saw the seeds, she wiped the flour from her hands.

She did not touch them.

“Where did you find those?” she asked.

“In the old storage shed.”

Grandmother looked at the blue thread.

“Your grandfather used that thread.”

“For what?” Luna asked.

“For things he did not want to lose.”

Luna smiled.

“Then why did he hide them?”

Grandmother looked toward the window.

“Maybe he didn't hide them. Maybe he was waiting for the right person to find them.”

Luna placed the seeds on the table.

“Can we plant one?”

Grandmother shook her head.

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“Because we don't know what they are.”

Her father laughed.

“Your grandmother is right. A farmer should know what he puts in the soil.”

“But the plant in the garden came from one of them, didn't it?” Luna asked.

Grandmother looked at her carefully.

“Perhaps.”

At that moment, there was a loud crash outside.

Everyone ran to the garden.

The old wooden fence had fallen over.

The horse was standing beside it, looking offended.

“He pushed it,” the goose announced.

The horse snorted.

“The goose frightened me.”

The goose honked angrily.

“I did not!”

Luna started laughing.

For a moment, the strange mystery disappeared behind an ordinary farm argument.

Her father repaired the fence while her mother gathered the vegetables that had been scattered on the ground.

Luna returned to the little plant.

Something had appeared beside it.

A tiny hole.

She knelt down.

Inside the hole was a single seed.

It had the silver bird mark.

Luna picked it up.

The moment it touched her hand, she heard a sound.

Not a voice.

Not music.

It sounded like wind passing through many trees at once.

Then she saw something in her mind.

A field covered in flowers.

A young woman carrying a basket.

A little boy running beside her.

An old tree near a river.

And twelve seeds lying on a wooden table.

The vision disappeared.

Luna opened her eyes.

Garo was staring at her.

“You saw something,” he said.

Luna nodded.

“I think these seeds belonged to someone before Grandfather.”

She took the seed back to Grandmother.

Grandmother held it for a moment.

Her expression changed.

“This one has the bird.”

“What does that mean?” Luna asked.

“I don't know.”

“You really don't know?”

Grandmother smiled.

“There are things I know, Luna. There are things I remember. And there are things your grandfather never told me.”

Luna looked at the twelve seeds.

“Then perhaps we should learn together.”

Grandmother nodded.

“Yes.”

That evening, Luna placed the seeds beside the little chest.

She counted them carefully.

One.

Two.

Three.

All the way to twelve.

Then she noticed something strange.

The seed with the chest symbol was gone.

Luna searched the table.

Nothing.

She searched the floor.

Nothing.

Garo entered the room.

He sniffed the air.

Then he looked toward the window.

Luna followed his gaze.

Outside, beneath the old tree, something small was shining.

Luna ran downstairs.

The seed was lying beside the tree.

But it was no longer brown.

A tiny green line had appeared across its surface.

Luna carefully picked it up.

At once, the old tree gave a soft creak.

One of its branches moved even though there was no wind.

A single leaf fell into Luna's hand.

On the leaf was the same silver mark.

Luna looked at Garo.

“I think the farm is trying to tell us something.”

Garo wagged his tail.

“Then we should listen.”

Luna looked at the seed again.

She did not plant it.

Not yet.

She had learned that some mysteries became clearer when you gave them time.

And somewhere beneath the old tree, something was waiting.

Something connected to the twelve strange seeds.

Something Grandfather had known about.

Something Luna would soon have to discover.
