Story 19

The First Planting

For three days, Luna did not plant the strange seed.

She kept it beside the little wooden chest and watched the tiny green line across its surface.

Every morning, the line was a little longer.

Grandmother told her not to hurry.

“A seed is not a button,” she said. “You cannot press it and expect an answer.”

Luna laughed.

“Then how do I know when it is ready?”

“You listen.”

“To the seed?”

“To everything around it.”

Luna tried.

She listened to the wind moving through the trees.

She listened to the pond.

She listened to the chickens scratching the earth and the horse breathing in the stable.

Most of all, she listened to the garden.

On the fourth morning, something was different.

The birds were gathered around the old tree.

The rabbit was sitting near the vegetable patch.

Even the cat had left its favorite sunny stone.

All of them were looking toward Luna.

Garo ran toward her.

“Today,” he said.

Luna looked at the seed.

The green line had opened into the shape of a tiny leaf.

She carried it downstairs.

Grandmother was waiting at the kitchen table.

“You heard them too,” Luna said.

Grandmother nodded.

“I did.”

“Where should I plant it?”

Grandmother pointed toward the garden.

“Choose the place yourself.”

Luna walked slowly between the rows.

She considered the pond.

She considered the old tree.

She considered the place where the metal box had been buried.

Then she stopped near the wooden bridge.

There was a small empty patch beside it.

It received sunlight in the morning and shade in the afternoon.

The soil was soft.

“Here,” Luna said.

Her father brought her a small shovel.

Her mother brought a clay cup filled with water.

Grandmother came last.

Even the animals seemed to understand that something important was about to happen.

Luna made a small hole in the soil.

She placed the seed inside.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Luna waited.

Then the earth moved.

Not violently.

It simply rose beneath her fingers.

A tiny crack appeared.

Garo stepped backward.

The horse gave a quiet sound from the stable.

The birds flew from the tree.

Then a green shoot appeared.

Luna smiled.

She poured a little water over it.

The shoot grew another inch.

Then another.

Within a few minutes, it had become a small plant.

Its leaves were unlike the leaves of any ordinary plant on the farm.

One was shaped like a bird.

Another looked like a wave.

The third looked almost like a tiny hand.

Luna touched the third leaf.

A warm feeling moved through her fingers.

Suddenly she heard many voices.

“Water.”

“Sun.”

“Careful.”

“Do not break it.”

“Give it space.”

The animals were speaking at once.

Luna laughed.

“One at a time!”

The rabbit hopped closer.

“It is thirsty.”

The goose shook its feathers.

“It needs more water.”

The old horse disagreed.

“Too much water can harm a young plant.”

Garo looked at Luna.

“The horse is right.”

Luna smiled.

“Then we will listen to the horse.”

She poured only a little more water.

That afternoon, the family returned to their ordinary work.

Her father repaired a section of the barn roof.

Her mother prepared vegetables for dinner.

Grandmother sat beneath the old tree and continued repairing her basket.

Luna stayed near the new plant.

She expected something magical to happen.

Nothing did.

And that surprised her.

The plant simply stood there.

Quiet.

Small.

Alive.

By sunset, Luna began to understand.

Not every magical thing needed to be spectacular.

Sometimes magic was simply the beginning of something.

That evening, Grandmother joined her beside the bridge.

“What are you thinking?” she asked.

“I thought something enormous would happen.”

Grandmother smiled.

“Maybe it already has.”

Luna looked at the plant.

“It is just a plant.”

“For now.”

They sat quietly.

After a while, Luna noticed a tiny mark on the soil.

It was shaped like a circle with four small lines around it.

The same symbol appeared on the little chest.

“Grandmother.”

She pointed toward the ground.

Grandmother's expression became serious.

“Do not touch it.”

Luna stopped immediately.

“Why?”

“Because your grandfather once told me something about that mark.”

“What did he say?”

Grandmother looked toward the river.

“He said the first planting was never about growing a plant.”

Luna waited.

“Then what was it about?”

Grandmother stood up.

“Finding out what the land remembers.”

Before Luna could ask another question, a breeze moved across the farm.

The new plant bent toward the river.

Its leaves shimmered in the fading light.

Then one leaf slowly unfolded.

Hidden underneath was a tiny golden seed.

Luna stared.

There had been no seed there before.

Grandmother whispered, “Now we have a new question.”

Luna carefully looked at the golden seed.

She did not touch it.

Not yet.

Somewhere beyond the river, the first star appeared in the evening sky.

And beneath it, the little plant continued to grow.
