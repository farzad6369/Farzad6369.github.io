Story 20

The Plant That Grew Overnight

That night, Luna could not sleep.

She kept thinking about the tiny golden seed hidden beneath the leaf of the new plant.

She had promised Grandmother that she would not touch it.

So she did not.

Instead, she watched it from her bedroom window.

The moon moved slowly across the sky.

The farm became quiet.

Even Garo eventually stopped barking at distant sounds and curled up near the kitchen door.

Luna was about to close the curtains when she saw something unusual.

The plant beside the wooden bridge was glowing.

Not brightly.

It gave off a soft golden light, like a candle hidden behind leaves.

Luna put on her shoes and went downstairs.

Garo immediately lifted his head.

“I know,” Luna whispered.

They walked outside together.

The night air was cool.

The river sounded louder than usual.

When Luna reached the plant, she stopped.

It had changed.

During the few hours since sunset, it had grown almost as tall as her waist.

Its leaves were larger.

Its stem had become strong.

And several small branches had appeared.

Garo stared at it.

“That is not normal.”

“I know.”

A tiny bird landed on one of the branches.

“It grew while everyone slept,” the bird said.

Luna looked at the plant.

“Why?”

The bird shook its feathers.

“It was waiting.”

“For what?”

Before the bird could answer, the plant moved.

One branch slowly turned toward the farmhouse.

Another pointed toward the river.

A third leaned toward the old tree.

And the last branch pointed toward the forest.

Luna remembered the four lines on the strange symbol.

Four directions.

Four paths.

She suddenly understood that the plant was not simply growing.

It was showing them something.

Garo walked around it.

“There are four branches.”

“Yes.”

“And four directions.”

“Yes.”

“I don't like where this is going.”

Luna laughed softly.

“Neither do I.”

At that moment, Grandmother appeared at the farmhouse door.

She was holding a lantern.

She did not look surprised.

“I wondered when it would happen,” she said.

Luna turned.

“You knew it would grow overnight?”

Grandmother walked closer.

“Your grandfather told me about the possibility.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because I wanted to know what you would do without being told.”

Luna looked at the plant.

“And?”

Grandmother smiled.

“You waited. You watched. You didn't force it.”

“Was that a test?”

“Perhaps.”

Garo sighed.

“Humans have too many tests.”

Grandmother laughed.

“You are right, Garo.”

The three of them stood beside the plant.

Grandmother pointed to the four branches.

“Your grandfather called this the Four-Way Plant.”

Luna looked surprised.

“He knew it existed?”

“He saw one once.”

“Where?”

“Here.”

Luna looked around the farm.

“But where did it go?”

Grandmother touched one of the leaves.

“It disappeared after one night.”

“Why?”

“Because the person who planted it made the wrong choice.”

Luna became serious.

“What choice?”

Grandmother looked toward the four directions.

“The plant shows four possible paths. But only one path should be followed at the beginning.”

“How do we know which one?”

Grandmother shook her head.

“I don't know.”

Luna smiled.

“Then we'll find out.”

Suddenly, the branch pointing toward the river began to glow.

The golden light became brighter.

A small flower opened at its tip.

Inside the flower was something that looked like a tiny piece of glass.

Luna leaned closer.

The glass reflected the moonlight.

Then it showed an image.

Luna saw the river.

She saw the old wooden bridge.

She saw a place farther downstream.

There was a large stone beside the water.

Someone had carved four circles into it.

The image disappeared.

Luna looked at Grandmother.

“The river.”

Grandmother nodded.

“It seems so.”

Garo stood up.

“Then we go tomorrow.”

Grandmother smiled.

“Tomorrow.”

Luna looked at the plant one last time.

It was already changing again.

The four branches were slowly folding toward the center.

The golden flower closed.

Then the entire plant became still.

By sunrise, it looked like an ordinary young tree.

But Luna knew it was not ordinary.

She returned to the house with Grandmother and Garo.

Before going upstairs, she looked back through the window.

The first light of morning touched the leaves.

For a moment, Luna thought she saw four tiny golden dots shining on the branches.

Then they disappeared.

Grandmother placed a hand on Luna's shoulder.

“Your first planting is complete.”

Luna smiled.

“And now?”

Grandmother looked toward the river.

“Now we find out what the farm has been waiting for.”

Luna went upstairs and placed the little chest beside her bed.

For the first time, she did not feel afraid of the mystery.

She felt ready.

Outside, the farm woke slowly.

The chickens began calling.

The horse stamped its foot.

The geese argued beside the pond.

Garo waited by the door.

And beyond the fields, the river continued moving toward a place Luna had never seen.

The adventure was only beginning.
